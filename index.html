A model that predicts a diagnosis correctly but cannot say why is of limited use to the person who has to act on it. That gap is what I work on.

Research

My published work applies feature attribution to clinical prediction: chronic kidney disease and multi-class obesity classification, both using SHAP and LIME to expose what drives a prediction. The limitation became clear quickly. Attribution tells you which inputs a model weighted. It does not tell you whether the model learned anything a physician would recognise as reasoning.

My current work approaches this from the generative side. ClinDiffXAI conditions a diffusion model on clinical concepts to synthesise dermoscopic skin lesion images, so the concepts a model depends on can be varied deliberately and inspected, rather than inferred after the fact. It also addresses a practical constraint: dermoscopic datasets are small, imbalanced, and skewed toward lighter skin tones.

Alongside this I am interested in empirical software engineering. Three and a half years of shipping production systems under client deadlines has left me with a stock of questions about how software actually gets built, and those questions are studiable.
