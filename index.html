About

I am a software engineer in Dhaka. Outside work, I do research in generative AI and computer vision with academic collaborators. My recent study used a concept-conditioned diffusion model to test whether explanations of image classifiers are faithful, with melanoma classification as the test case. In this setting, TCAV failed a negative control, and observational attribution misranked the concepts the classifiers relied on. Before that, I published two papers on explainable machine learning for clinical data.

Three and a half years of building production software for banks and an Azure telemetry platform made me interested in how software is built and tested, and I want to study it. In my PhD, I want to build generative and vision models, and methods that check whether AI systems behave as their explanations claim. My long-term goal is an academic career in Bangladesh.

Featured research

Validating concept-level audits of melanoma classifiers with diffusion counterfactuals and matched positive controls

Md. Shohanoor Rahman *, Md. Hasibul Islam, Md Kishor Morol, Moinul Hossain, Dip Nandi, Liew Tze Hui

Under review, Scientific Reports.* First and corresponding author.

Question. If only one clinical sign in a dermoscopy image changes, how does a melanoma classifier’s output change, and do common explanation methods agree?

  • 38classifiers, 31 of them controls with a known answer
  • 16 / 16matched controls passed
  • 12 / 12independently labelled controls passed (3 concepts)
  • +2.77logits: pigmentation effect

What I did

  • Trained a concept-conditioned DDPM (128 px, ISIC 2020) in which each of the seven 7-point checklist concepts is a knob from 0 to 1, while the rest of the image stays fixed.
  • Found a signal-leak bias in the start noise (R² = 0.90) and removed it with a colour-calibrated start noise: colour outliers fell from 78.5–84.8% to 6.6–13.3%. An independent evaluator found 4 of the 7 knobs specific (pigment network, vascular structures, pigmentation, regression structures).
  • Trained 38 classifiers: 6 ISIC 2020 melanoma classifiers (ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B0, ViT-B/16, two seeds each), 1 Derm7pt classifier and 31 controls with a known answer.
  • Fixed each decision rule in code before its run and reported the rule that failed: quartile-based controls were confounded. Matched controls then passed 16 of 16. Because those labels also conditioned the generator, I repeated the test with independently labelled controls under a rule fingerprinted with SHA-256 before the run: 12 of 12 passed for three concepts. Regression had too few pairs to test.

Findings

  • In this setting, two TCAV implementations (mine and Captum) failed a permuted-label negative control (0.834 and 0.889 against a limit of 0.25). Part of the failure traced to the network architecture. Captum 0.9.0 misreports CAV accuracy; newer Captum code handles this.
  • Observational attribution passed the controls but ranked vascular structures first for all six melanoma classifiers. The validated audit found a large pigmentation effect (+2.77 logits) and small vascular (+0.29) and pigment-network (+0.05) effects.

Limits. 128 px images; one main training dataset (ISIC 2020); no dermatologist rated the generated images; regression structures validated only with the generator’s own labels; the TCAV result holds for this setting.
